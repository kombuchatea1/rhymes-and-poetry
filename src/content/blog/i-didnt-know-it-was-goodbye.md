Sometimes an old photo stops you in your tracks. The person in it is you, but the moment feels like it belongs to someone else. This poem is about that feeling: grateful for where you are, yet wistful for the goodbyes you never knew you were saying.

<div class="poem-block">
A somber reflection<br>
Of an unrecognizable time<br>
A photo from the distant past<br>
Hard to fathom it was mine<br>
<br>
I can't believe it's come and gone<br>
Surely, there's brighter days ahead<br>
Yet I can't imagine I'd be here<br>
Without the memories I replay in my head<br>
<br>
I wouldn't want to go back<br>
But sometimes I'm saddened within<br>
Because I didn't know it was goodbye<br>
I just wish I had soaked it all in
</div>

## The Goodbyes We Never Knew We Were Saying

Nostalgia has a way of sneaking up on us. One moment you're scrolling through your phone or cleaning out a drawer, and the next you're staring at an old photo of someone who looks like you but feels like a stranger. That's the heart of this poem: the strange distance between who we were and who we've become, and the quiet ache of realizing that some chapters closed without any warning.

What makes these memories bittersweet isn't that we want to go back. Most of us wouldn't trade the growth, the lessons, or the people we've found along the way. It's that so many last moments didn't feel like last moments at the time. The last day at a childhood home, the last carefree summer with old friends, the last ordinary afternoon before life changed. We were simply living, not knowing we were saying goodbye.

If there's a lesson here, it's to slow down and soak it in now. Today will one day be a memory you replay in your head. Take the photo, linger at the table a little longer, and notice the small things while they're still happening. Gratitude for the present is the best way to honor the past, and the best way to make sure tomorrow's look back holds a little less regret.
